Interpretation

By August 2025, U-3 was 4.30%, near February 2020's 3.83% and the 2019 average of 3.78%, consistent with broad recovery. U-1 was 1.42%, just 0.13 percentage points above February 2020 and 0.12 percentage points above 2019, so it signals little remaining long-term unemployment slack. LFPR was 63.34%, 0.47 points below February 2020 and 0.10 below 2019; population aging may explain part of the gap. U-1 agrees with U-3, while LFPR is a slightly softer signal, illustrating Gilchrist and Hobijn's point that slack measures can diverge.

Methods

Monthly estimates use WTFINL person weights. U-1 is unemployed for 15 or more weeks as a percent of the labor force. LFPR is the labor force as a percent of the population age 16 and older.
